# The Belief Bridge

**By [Kuber Sharma](https://kubersharma.com)** · Full specification at [kubersharma.com/frameworks/belief-bridge](https://kubersharma.com/frameworks/belief-bridge)

---

## The problem

Positioning is the art of changing what a buyer believes. Most positioning advice assumes the buyer can already picture the outcome. In enterprise AI, buyers cannot picture the outcome at all, because the outcome is something nobody has bought before.

You cannot describe your way across that gap. You have to build a bridge.

---

## The four planks

### Plank 01: Evidence · *"it works for someone"*

Hard, verifiable, on-the-record. Named customer contacts. Benchmarks with the test rig. A working demo the buyer can interrupt. Evidence is the first plank and the only one that cannot be borrowed.

### Plank 02: Analogy · *"it is like X, but for Y"*

The familiar shape the new thing fits inside. The right analogy is one the buyer has lived through (CRM, the cloud, the smartphone). The wrong analogy is one their CFO has never bought (the metaverse, web3, the previous wave of AI).

### Plank 03: Proof Point · *"it would work for me"*

The closest reference customer to the buyer's own situation, by industry, size, and stage. Generic logos do not count. A bank cannot be sold by a retail proof point. A 5,000-person org cannot be sold by a 50-person one.

### Plank 04: Transfer · *"I can repeat it tomorrow"*

The sentence the buyer can take into their next meeting and have their boss nod. The plank vendors most often skip. If your pitch requires the buyer to translate it into their own words, you have not built the fourth plank, and the deal will die in the buyer's quiet moment between meetings.

---

## The key insight

Most GTM motions build three planks. The fourth is the one buyers need and vendors most often forget. **The deal does not die in your meeting. It dies in their next one.**

---

## Where it came from

Named in 2022, in a hotel room in San Francisco the night before a Tableau Conference keynote. The four planks had been forming across different decks for months without a name. Putting them in order, and naming the order, was the work.
