# The Trust Architecture

**By [Kuber Sharma](https://kubersharma.com)** · Full specification at [kubersharma.com/frameworks/trust-architecture](https://kubersharma.com/frameworks/trust-architecture)

---

## The problem

Every enterprise AI pitch leads with capability. The model can do this. The model can do that.

The buyer has already accepted that the capability is real. The buyer is asking three different questions, in a specific order. The vendor who answers them in the wrong order loses the deal.

---

## The three layers

### Layer 01: Technical Trust

*"Will this actually do the thing you say it does, reliably, at our scale?"*

The easiest layer. Demo videos answer this. Reference architectures answer this. Benchmarks answer this. Technical Trust is necessary and the most commonly mistaken-for-sufficient.

### Layer 02: Operational Trust

*"Can my team actually run this when it breaks at 3am?"*

The middle layer. Can the operations team you already have, with the tools you already use, support this product when it fails? Most enterprise AI fails here, after the technical demo passes. Vendors who pitch capability without operations lose at this layer.

### Layer 03: Strategic Trust

*"Will my career survive it if this goes wrong?"*

The deepest layer. The one nobody puts on a slide. If your product makes a mistake, is the buyer the one explaining it to the CFO? Vendors who do not address this layer are negotiating with a buyer who has already mentally said no.

---

## The key insight

The layers are sequential, not parallel. A buyer who has not received Technical Trust will not engage with Operational Trust. A buyer with Technical Trust but not Operational Trust will not hear the Strategic Trust pitch.

**Buyers evaluate bottom to top. Vendors pitch top to bottom. That is the mismatch.**

---

## Where it came from

A two-layer version originated at Microsoft in 2017, selling Azure Machine Learning to enterprises with no path to production. The third layer (Strategic Trust) became visible at Salesforce in 2020, watching a year of deals stall not on the model but on the executive sponsor's calendar. The full three-layer version has been in use at UiPath since 2022.
